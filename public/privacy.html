Tracknotes Privacy Policy

Last updated: 31 October 2026

Tracknotes is a hiking guide app for long-distance trails, mostly in Australia, with built-in guides for New Zealand, the United States and Japan, and the option to import routes from anywhere. It is designed to work offline-first and to collect as little personal information as possible.

In the app you can share information with other users, such as adding new waypoints or commenting on waypoints. Our principle for anything you choose to share publicly is that it is in the public domain, but you still control what is available in our app: you can delete anything you have shared, edit shared waypoints and community routes, and download community routes. Because shared content is in the public domain, copies others have already made may remain. The details are below.

What we collect

No account, no email, no password. Tracknotes has no sign-up. The first time you post a comment, share something or report something, the app registers an anonymous device identity: a random device ID and the display name you choose. The display name is the only identity shown to other users.

Content you post. Comments, water reports, and photos you attach to waypoints, or to OpenStreetMap points of interest (shops, cafés, campgrounds and the like) shown in the app, are stored on our servers and shown publicly to other users of the app alongside your display name and the time of posting (for a water report, also the time you say you saw the water).

Photo location. Photos are uploaded as taken: the app re-encodes them at a reduced quality to make them smaller, but does not remove their metadata. A photo may therefore keep its EXIF data, including the GPS location where it was taken if your camera recorded one, and anyone who views or downloads the photo can read it. This is deliberate, so other hikers can see exactly where a photo was taken, as this is often useful information. If you do not want a photo’s location published, turn off location tagging in your camera app before taking it, or do not attach it.

Waypoints you share (optional). You can add your own waypoints to a trail on the phone. A waypoint you keep to yourself (“Only me”) stays on your device and is never sent to us. A waypoint you share with everyone is stored on our servers and published: every user of the app sees it on that trail, and anyone can read it through our public API. What is published is its position, name, type and note, your display name, and when it was added and last edited. You can edit it, make it private again, or delete it at any time; either of the last two takes it off the app and the public API, but our copy is kept, marked deleted, and our admins can still see it for moderation. A private waypoint can still reach us inside a plan, if you choose it as a stop (see Plans).

Location. Your GPS position is used on-device to show where you are on the trail and estimate arrival times. It is never sent to our servers on its own. Places you have been or plan to be do reach us when you choose to send them: a shared waypoint placed with “Use my location” is stored at that position; a community route you share is a track you may have walked and recorded, and its original GPX file, if you include it, can hold the times you walked it; and a synced plan names the places you plan to stop at and how far along the trail they are. Background location tracking is off by default and only runs if you turn it on in Settings. Check-in sharing composes a message with your position that is sent only through apps you choose in the system share sheet.

Plans. Once the app has a device identity (however it was created, including by filing a report), the day plans you make for the built-in trails are copied to our servers so they survive a reinstall and can be opened in a linked browser. A plan holds its name, direction, start date, the stops you chose with their nights, notes and “booked” ticks, the resupply points and alternate routes you picked, and when it was last changed. A stop carries the place’s name and its distance along the trail, and that includes a private waypoint of your own if you choose it as a stop. A plan is private to your account unless you share it (below). Your walking pace and hours per day are not part of it and stay on the device. Plans for trails you imported yourself, and for community routes, stay on your device.

Shared plan links (optional). If you share a plan, we create a link with a long random address. Anyone who has the link can see the plan and your display name; it is not listed or searchable anywhere. You can stop sharing at any time, after which the link no longer works.

Linked browsers (optional). You can link a web browser to your phone’s identity by typing a short code the phone shows (Settings → Linked browsers), so the website can sync your plans and share routes. For each linked browser we keep a sign-in token (stored on our side only as a one-way hash), the label it was linked under (prefilled from the browser and operating system, such as “Chrome on macOS”, and editable), and when it was linked and last used. A linked browser stays signed in for 180 days after it was last used, and you can unlink it from the phone or from the browser at any time. An unlinked or lapsed browser can no longer sign in, but its record (the label and those times) is kept, marked revoked. On the website, the sign-in token, your plans and any trails you import are kept in that browser’s own storage.

Network addresses. When the app registers a new device identity, and when a browser enters a link code, we record the network address the request came from (an IPv4 address, or for IPv6 the network prefix, the first half of the address) with the time, so that one network cannot register accounts or guess link codes without limit. These records hold no account ID and are deleted soon after they are 24 hours old.

No analytics, no advertising. The app contains no analytics SDKs, ad networks, or third-party trackers.

Community routes (optional). If you choose to share an imported route with the community, we store the processed route (track, waypoints, distances and elevation), the name, description, credit line and country/region you enter, your display name at the time, and, if you include it, the original GPX file you imported (on the website, untick “Include the original GPX file” to leave it out). The route, its text and your display name are published publicly under CC0 (public domain) on the website and in the app. The points of interest we add to a shared route from OpenStreetMap (see Searching OpenStreetMap below) are not part of that release: they are © OpenStreetMap contributors, under the Open Database Licence, and the published route credits them as such. The original GPX file is kept privately, for re-processing only, under an unguessable address that is never linked or shown publicly. If a route is hidden by moderation, its published copy is taken down until it is restored. Sharing is opt-in: an import you do not share never leaves your device. The published route carries no recording times: timestamps are dropped from the processed route before it is stored or published. A raw GPX file can still hold them, along with your name or device, which is why it is kept only privately and is yours to leave out.

Automated review of community routes. Each shared route is checked automatically, and its text, region, summary statistics, waypoint names, a sample of its waypoint descriptions, the names of its alternate routes and side trips, and a sample of its coordinates are sent to Anthropic's API (the Claude model) for an automated review that helps our admins spot spam, abuse or routes that are not walking routes. Anthropic processes this data to return the review; it is not used to identify you. The review's result is visible to you and to our admins, not to the public.

Automated review of comments and shared waypoints. The text of every new comment, and the name and description of every new or edited shared waypoint, is checked by an automated safety filter (the Llama Guard model, run by Cloudflare, which already stores our data). When that filter flags a post, or when an established user reports it, the post is sent to Anthropic's API (the Claude model) for a review: for a comment, its text, its water status and the time you say you saw the water, how many photos it has and the place it is on; for a shared waypoint, its name, type, description and position; and, for either, the reasons and notes of the reports filed against it. Your display name, your account and your photos are not sent. A post the review finds clearly abusive, spam or otherwise harmful is hidden at once until an admin looks at it. The review's result is visible only to our admins. Anthropic processes this data to return the review; it is not used to identify you.

Searching OpenStreetMap (points of interest). To find the shops, water, campsites and other places near a route, three features send the route’s outline (a line of coordinates along it, never your name, device or account) to public OpenStreetMap search servers (the Overpass API), which are run by third parties under their own terms and see the outline and the network address it came from:

  • Shared community routes. Our server sends the outline of each published community route, after it is published, to these servers, one after another if one does not answer: overpass-api.de (run by FOSSGIS e.V., Germany), overpass.private.coffee (Private.coffee, Austria) and overpass.kumi.systems (Kumi Systems, Austria). The places found are added to the published route. Only routes that are already public are sent.
  • “Find places along this route” in the app. When you ask for it on a guide you imported, your phone sends that guide’s outline directly to the same three servers, tried in that order. Nothing goes through our server.
  • Points of interest for a trail imported on the website. When you ask for them, your browser sends the outline directly to overpass-api.de, or to overpass.kumi.systems if that does not answer. Nothing goes through our server.

Filling in missing elevation (optional). When you import a GPX file without elevation and choose to fill it in (on the app’s import screen, or the website’s “Fetch elevation” button), your device sends the track’s coordinates directly to the public Open-Elevation service (api.open-elevation.com), which returns the terrain height at each. Nothing goes through our server.

Where data is stored

On your device: downloaded maps, trail guides, your settings, favorites, routes, plans, your waypoints (private and shared), and a cached copy of comments and shared waypoints (so the app works offline).

On our servers: posted comments, water reports, photos, shared waypoints, plans and their share links, display names, linked-browser records, moderation reports, the short-lived network-address records above, and any community routes you share (with their original GPX file, when you include it). These are stored with Cloudflare (D1 database and R2 object storage). We request Oceania placement and it is where our storage currently runs, but Cloudflare treats placement as best-effort, so data may be stored or replicated in other regions.

Moderation and reporting

Every comment, every shared waypoint and every community route has a Report action. Reports are reviewed and content that is abusive, unsafe, or spam is removed. A comment or shared waypoint that an established user reports is sent to the automated review above, and hidden at once if the review finds it clearly abusive, spam or harmful; one that several established users report is hidden whatever the review found, as is an unverified community route that several established users report. Hidden content stays hidden until an admin looks at it. Repeat abuse may result in a device being blocked from posting.

Deleting your data

Settings → Delete account (on the phone) anonymises your device identity on our servers: your display name is replaced with “Deleted user”, and the phone and every linked browser are signed out for good. It also deletes every comment you posted, every waypoint you shared and every plan you saved (they disappear from all devices on their next sync), including ones that were already deleted or hidden:

  • Comments: the text, water status and observation time are erased, and your uploaded photos are deleted.
  • Plans: the plan’s contents are erased and any shared plan link is removed, so it stops working.
  • Shared waypoints: the name, note and position are erased, and the waypoint is unshared for good (moderation cannot restore it). On your phone they are kept as private waypoints.

What stays in our database for each is an empty record: its ID, the trail (and waypoint or place) it belonged to, a waypoint’s type, and when it was created, changed and deleted, so that devices which synced it know to drop it. Reports you filed are kept as a moderation record (the reason you chose and when), but any text you wrote in them is erased. This is immediate and irreversible.

A community route you shared can be deleted at any time from its page (Delete); this removes it from the public list and deletes the stored route and GPX file. We keep a record of the route, marked removed: its name, description, credit line, the display name it was shared under, its summary statistics and a fingerprint of its track, which lets moderation stop a removed route from being shared again. Because shared routes are released as CC0, copies others have already downloaded or saved may remain on their devices. Deleting your account leaves the routes you shared public but removes your display name from them: the stored route is rewritten without it and the earlier copies that named you are deleted. The original GPX file, if you included one, is deleted too. The description and credit line you wrote stay published. Delete the routes first if you want them gone. Local data on your own device (favorites, routes, downloaded maps) stays on your device until you clear it or uninstall.

Children

Tracknotes is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from them.
